Aspect 2: CSS Architecture

A Utility-Class Model

The template has no style block and no stylesheet of its own. Appearance comes from W3.CSS classes plus 14 inline style attributes. For example, the navigation bar carries six classes, and each one contributes a declaration or two.

Decoding the navigation bar classes (w3.css 6)
ClassDeclarationEffect
w3-flexdisplay:flexBrand and links sit in one row
w3-topposition:fixed; top:0Bar stays at the top
w3-wideletter-spacing:4pxWidely spaced letters
w3-paddingpadding:8px 16pxSpace inside the bar
w3-cardbox-shadowSoft shadow under the bar

Selector Types in the Framework

  • Element: h1{font-size:36px}
  • Class: carries nearly everything else
  • Universal: *,*:before,*:after{box-sizing:inherit}
  • Pseudo-class and descendant: .w3-striped tbody tr:nth-child(even)

Cascade and Critique

W3.CSS 6 marks many utilities !important, so a utility wins over ordinary rules. That is why an inline style cannot beat .w3-padding but can beat .w3-content. Several inline styles also duplicate built-in classes, and I recommend replacing them.
